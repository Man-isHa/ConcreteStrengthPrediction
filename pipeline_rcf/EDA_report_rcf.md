# RCF/RCP Concrete — EDA & Modeling Report

Pipeline over `Master_sheet_RCF.xlsx` (dataset extracted from ~104 research papers
on **recycled concrete fines / recycled concrete powder** used as a partial cement
replacement). Goal: predict **compressive strength** and the **optimal cement-replacement
level**.

Reproduce: `pip install openpyxl`, then run `pipeline_rcf/01_load_clean.py` →
`02_parse_explode.py` → `03_features.py` → `04_model.py` → `05_plots.py` →
`06_ablation.py` → `07_best_model.py` → `08_report_figs.py`. Artifacts land in
`pipeline_rcf/out/`.

> Every number below is regenerated by the scripts. The companion LaTeX report is
> `../rcf_report.tex`.

---

## 1. What the dataset actually is

The workbook has **two** sheets, and that is the single most important structural
fact about it.

- **`Sheet1`** — 225 columns × 105 data rows, under a **four-row hierarchical
  header**: super-banner → category → feature (55 features) → sub-field. Each
  feature occupies four columns (`Extracted Value` / `Unit` / `Source Type` /
  `Exact Evidence`); only the first two are predictive. The first five columns are
  single-level paper metadata (`Paper Title`, `Authors`, `Journal Name`,
  `Publication Year`, `DOI`). One paper appears twice under different
  capitalisation, so there are **104 unique papers**.
- **`Experimental Matrix`** — a flat 6-column × 1162-row table that is **already
  exploded to one row per mix**: `Paper Title, Mix ID, Replacement Level,
  Activation Method, Key Processing Parameters, Reported Strength`.

Two RCF-specific traps:

1. **Missing values are the literal string `"NULL"`**, very often with a trailing
   parenthetical (`NULL (Note: the paper reports w/c ...)`). An equality test
   against `"NULL"` keeps those as data.
2. **Units are inconsistent within a single column.** `Specific Surface Area`
   alone appears as m²/kg, m²/g and cm²/g; `Bulk Density` as kg/m³ and g/cm³; the
   strength column includes one `psi` paper and one `kg/cm²` paper. Values must be
   converted before any plausibility filter is applied.

## 2. The key data-representation insight

Sheet1's `Compressive Strength` cell packs a **(replacement level × curing age)
grid** — and sometimes a third **w/b** axis — into free text, in ~16 layouts:

```
Ref (0%):                  w/b 0.4:              3-Day: 33.2 (MA20), 18.1 (MA50), ...
2 days: 26.6 ± 0.89        0% RP: ~48.5          7-Day: 48.1 (MA20), 33.0 (MA50), ...
28 days: 45.8 ± 1.45       15% RP: ~47.3
```

`02_parse_explode.py` resolves, for every value, a replacement level and (where
stated) a curing age, from an inline qualifier, a segment header, or persistent
line context. It reads the **two sources into one schema** and de-duplicates on
the measurement.

**Yield: 1825 data points across 85 of the 102 in-scope papers** — 976 from the
Sheet1 grids, 849 from the Experimental Matrix. **1284 points over 57 papers**
also resolve a curing age; 384 are at the standard 28-day age.

Precision safeguards:

- unsigned number matching (a leading `-` is always a separator);
- **the strength is only ever read from the value zone of an item.** This is not a
  detail: in `R20: 48.1` and `N-0.50-0: 28.5` the mix label contains numbers, and
  reading the item as a whole makes the parser report a strength of 20 and 0.50.
  Items are split into `label: value` or `value (label)` first;
- a **per-paper whitelist** — a resolved level must match one of the paper's
  declared `Cement Replacement Level(s)`, plus 0 for the control, which rejects
  mis-read mix IDs (`MFP400`, `Series 7`);
- unit conversion (psi ×0.00689476, kg/cm² ×0.0980665) and sanity clamps
  (0 ≤ % ≤ 100, 0 < age ≤ 400 d, 0.5 ≤ MPa ≤ 250).

**Two papers are excluded as out of scope** (`out/excluded_papers.csv`): both
replace *aggregate*, not cement, so their "replacement level" means something
different from the rest of the corpus.

**Curing age is not assumed.** Only 458 of the 1162 matrix rows state an age.
Those rows are kept with `age_known = False` rather than being silently treated as
28-day; Study 6 tests that assumption empirically instead.

## 3. Feature engineering (`03_features.py`)

- Numeric cells → mean of the numbers, **after** unit normalisation and inside a
  physical range guard. The guard is load-bearing: the w/b cell
  `RC: 0.41 / C30R: 0.45 / C100R: 0.48` contains 30 and 100 from the mix labels,
  and an unguarded mean reports a water/binder ratio of 26.
- Free text → compact categoricals: `activation_method`
  {none, thermal, **carbonation**, mechanical, chemical, multiple, other} —
  carbonation gets its own level because CO₂ curing is a central RCF treatment;
  `processing_method` {crushing, milling, thermal, combined, other};
  `material_family` {powder, fines, paste, cdw, aac, other}, which folds the 90
  free-text `Material Type` spellings together; `pozzolanic_reactivity` as an
  ordinal (negative markers tested first, so "Limited pozzolanic reactivity" does
  not score as high).
- Fill rates among the 85 yielding papers: `material_family` 100%,
  `processing_method` 97%, w/b 87%, CaO 72%, SiO₂ 68%, down to BET 8% and
  amorphous content 6% — i.e. **0–94% missing, with 18 of 30 features more than
  half missing**.
- **No imputation.** Trees handle NaN natively; imputers are fit only inside a CV
  split (Study 4).

## 4. Within-paper checks on the parsed data

Cross-paper averages are meaningless here — the corpus runs from 0.6 MPa pastes to
a 184 MPa UHPC — so both checks are computed **within** a paper.

Normalising each *(paper, replacement level)* series by its own 28-day strength:
77% of pre-28-day points fall below the series' own 28-day value and 83% of later
points exceed it. The median ratio runs 0.62 (2 d) → 0.83 (7 d) → 1.00 (28 d) →
1.12 (90 d). The 1-day point (0.87, n = 106) sits above the 2- and 3-day points
and breaks the monotone pattern; it comes from a different subset of series, and
is a composition effect rather than a reversal.

The strength-vs-replacement shape is **strongly one-directional**, and this is the
biggest single contrast with the RHA corpus. Of the 31 papers reporting at least
three distinct levels at 28 days:

| shape | papers | share |
|---|---|---|
| peak at the **lowest** level tested | 22 | 71% |
| interior optimum | 7 | 23% |
| still rising at the highest level | 2 | 6% |

For RCF/RCP, replacing cement usually just costs strength. That is consistent with
the material: recycled concrete powder is far less reactive than rice husk ash, and
largely acts as a filler.

## 5. Modeling

Evaluation is **repeated grouped hold-out** throughout — `GroupShuffleSplit`, 30%
of *papers* held out, 40 repeats, reported as mean ± std. No paper is ever on both
sides of a split. `GroupKFold` is used only to generate out-of-fold predictions for
plotting; its pooled R² is optimistic and is labelled as such wherever it appears.

Three framings: **A** absolute strength; **B** strength relative to the paper's
control; **C** absolute strength *given* the paper's own 0% control strength at the
same age (always known in practice, so not leakage). Control rows are excluded from
B and C so the target cannot equal the feature.

Framing C has **823 blend rows over 44 papers**.

**A noise ceiling worth knowing about.** 180 of the 260 (paper, level, age) cells in
framing C contain more than one mix — different activation treatments at the same
replacement level and age. Their spread is irreducible for any model that does not
observe the treatment, and it caps R² at **0.80**.

Headline ablation results (full tables in `out/ablation.csv`):

| axis | winner | R² |
|---|---|---|
| S1 framing | C: blend given control | 0.341 (A −0.184, B −0.170) |
| S2 model | HistGB 0.384, ExtraTrees 0.371, RF/GBR 0.353, XGB 0.341 | Ridge −23, MLP −229 |
| S3 features | base + categorical 0.373 (base alone 0.311) | text embeddings do not help |
| S4 imputation | native handling 0.341 | no imputer wins on equal repeats |
| S5 priors | monotone(age, control) 0.339 vs plain 0.311 | |
| S6 data | duplicate mixes averaged 0.388 | matrix-only 0.160 |

**Unlike the RHA corpus, adding features helps here.** With 44 papers rather than
23, chemistry and the canonicalised categoricals earn their place instead of
overfitting. Text embeddings (MiniLM and TF-IDF) still do not.

## 6. Bottom line

1. The parsing step is again the highest-leverage part of the pipeline — and the
   two parser bugs that mistook mix-label numbers for strengths cost 0.2 of R²
   until they were found. Any change to `02_parse_explode.py` should be re-checked
   against the raw cells by hand.
2. The binding constraint is **unobserved mix covariates**, not model capacity: the
   noise ceiling is 0.80 and several distinct activation treatments share a single
   (paper, level, age) coordinate.
3. Highest-leverage next steps: extract per-mix activation and w/b into structured
   columns (the Experimental Matrix has them as free text); recover the 445 logged
   parse failures, most of which are relative-only ("+18% at 3 days") or
   graph-only; capture base-mix descriptors (cement grade, aggregate,
   superplasticizer, mortar-vs-concrete).
